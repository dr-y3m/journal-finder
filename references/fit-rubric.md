# Fit rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 5 (half points allowed). Base the score on evidence seen in this run (the aims-and-scope page, author guidelines, editorials, recent issues), not on the journal's reputation. The overall fit is the mean of the five scores; below 3.0 the journal fails the gate.

## Scope: does the journal say it publishes this topic?

- **5**: the topic or its core constructs are named explicitly in the aims and scope.
- **4**: clearly inside a named area of the journal.
- **3**: inside the journal's broad remit, but the topic itself isn't named.
- **2**: adjacent; the editor would have to stretch the scope.
- **1**: tangential.
- **0**: outside the scope, or explicitly excluded.

## Conversation: is the journal already discussing this?

- **5**: three or more closely related papers in the last five years, or the journal is heavily cited in the manuscript and still publishes that stream.
- **4**: two closely related recent papers.
- **3**: one related recent paper, or a clearly adjacent stream.
- **2**: related only at the level of the broad field.
- **0–1**: nothing related found.

## Method: will the editor accept the design?

- **5**: the journal regularly publishes this method and design.
- **4**: publishes it sometimes, with no stated concerns.
- **3**: rarely publishes it; no stance found.
- **2**: guidelines express caution (e.g., require multi-source data, a CB-SEM comparison, or a longitudinal design).
- **1**: strongly discouraged.
- **0**: an editorial or guideline says such papers will be desk-rejected.

## Context: does the empirical setting suit the journal?

- **5**: the setting is central to the journal (a regional journal on the same region, an SME journal for an SME sample, a sector journal for the same sector).
- **3**: neutral.
- **1**: the journal expects something the paper lacks (multi-country data, a specific sector, archival data).

## Contribution: does the type and format of the paper match?

- **5**: matches the journal's preferred contribution type and its article format and length.
- **3**: acceptable with adjustments (cutting length, reframing implications).
- **1**: mismatch, e.g., a technical theory-testing paper for a practitioner outlet, a narrative review for a journal that accepts only systematic reviews, a purely conceptual paper for an empirical-only journal.

## Reading the mean

- **4.0 and above**: strong fit; a credible first choice at its prestige level.
- **3.0 to 3.9**: plausible; the report should say what to adjust (framing, length, positioning).
- **Below 3.0**: fails the gate and goes to Excluded with the reason.

Turn every dimension scored 2 or lower into a concrete desk-rejection risk in the report, e.g., "Method 2: guidelines require multi-source data and the sample is single-source; address common method bias head-on in the cover letter and methods section."
